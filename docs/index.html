Native macOS app

Turn a folder of photos into a video

SISR (Simple Image Sequence Renderer) is a free, open-source Mac app for photographers and creators. Open a numbered stills folder, preview the sequence, crop and grade, then export MP4, MOV, or GIF — with optional date overlays and sizes ready for Instagram, HD, or 4K.

Download for Mac Mac user guide Crops & framing

Prefer the native Mac app: SISR-native-*-macos-universal.zip on GitHub Releases (or the Mac App Store when listed). The classic Python build remains available too — see Which download? below.

Built for the Mac

Three panes: sequence & encode on the left, live viewer and filmstrip in the center, crop and color on the right — powered by SwiftUI and AVFoundation.

What you can do

Live preview & filmstrip

Scrub frames with a thumbnail filmstrip, set in/out points, and see crop and color updates as you work.

Crop, straighten, rotate

Interactive crop handles, straighten with a slider or typed degrees, 90° rotate, and quick sizes from 8K to 360p — including portrait for social.

Color & detail

Exposure, contrast, highlights, shadows, saturation, plus optional sharpen, noise reduction, and vignette — grouped so the inspector stays calm.

Encode with confidence

H.264 (and more) with auto bitrates sized for 1080p and 4K, or open Bitrate & quality to override. Deflicker analysis reports progress before you render.

Dates on every frame

Burn in capture date from camera metadata when available — useful for builds, gardens, and trips.

Framing you can predict

Presets for vertical social (9:16), HD, and 4K. Crop diagrams show how Keep top / Center / Keep bottom line up with the photo.

What the output can look like

Real still frames from SISR — overlays and crop presets as they appear on screen.

Who it is for

  • Photographers turning still sequences into short videos
  • Creators who want dates or frame numbers burned into the picture
  • Anyone who prefers a focused Mac app over configuring NLE export by hand

Get started

With the native app: open a folder of numbered stills (name_0001.jpg …), set size and encode options, then click Render.
